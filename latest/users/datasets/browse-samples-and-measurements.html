Browse samples and measurements#

A dataset’s page, a project’s page and a person’s or organization’s page each have a Samples tab and a Measurements tab. Each lists the records in a table, one type at a time. Use them to see what a dataset, a project or a contributor holds and to find one record in it. A sample’s page has a Measurements tab too, which shows every type at once.

Browse a dataset’s samples and measurements#

  1. Open the dataset’s page. See The dataset page.

  2. Open Samples or Measurements in the row of tabs under the header. You can also select the Samples or Measurements figure on the Overview tab, which links to the same tab.

  3. If the dataset holds more than one type, the heading is a menu. Select it and choose a type. The menu gives the number of records of each type, and the most numerous type is shown first.

  4. Search or filter the table. The search box in the toolbar looks in the fields the type’s registration lists for search. The Filters button opens the other filters of the type.

  5. Select a column heading to sort by it. Select a name to open the record.

A measurement’s second column is its sample. It is a link when you may open the sample, and a dash when you may not. A measurement can be made on a sample of another dataset.

Browse a project’s samples and measurements#

  1. Open the project’s page.

  2. Open Samples or Measurements in the row of tabs under the header, or select the matching figure on the Overview tab.

  3. The tab lists the records of all the project’s datasets that you may see. The column after the name gives each record’s dataset. It is a link when you may open the dataset.

  4. Choose a type, search, filter and sort as on a dataset’s tab. The Dataset filter narrows the list to the datasets you choose.

A project’s Measurements tab lists the measurements made in the project’s datasets, including those made on a sample of another project. That sample is not on the project’s Samples tab.

A project’s tabs are open to everyone who may open the project. If you may see no record in any of its datasets, because they are not published or are private, the tab says there is nothing to list, as it does for a project with no records.

Browse a person’s or an organization’s samples and measurements#

  1. Open the person’s or organization’s page.

  2. Open Samples or Measurements in the row of tabs under the header.

  3. Choose a type, search, filter and sort as on a dataset’s tab. The column after the name gives each record’s dataset, a link when you may open it.

The tabs list a record in these cases:

  • The person or organization is credited on the record itself.

  • They are credited on the record’s dataset.

  • They are credited on the project the record’s dataset is in.

  • For an organization, the project that holds the record’s dataset is owned by the organization.

Credit does not spread any further. Being credited on a sample does not list other people’s measurements of it, and an organization does not list the records its members are credited on. A record that qualifies in several ways is listed once.

Everyone sees the records that are published and public. A person who opens their own page also sees the records they hold a level on, such as those in a private dataset or an unpublished one, and the filters offer their own records. An organization’s page lists only published public records, even for the members of its team. A person or organization with nothing you may see gets the same message as one with no records.

A Measurements tab can list a measurement made in a published dataset on a sample whose own dataset is not published. The Sample column then shows a dash.

Browse a sample’s measurements#

  1. Open the sample’s page. See The sample page.

  2. Open Measurements in the row of tabs under the header, or select the Measurements figure or See all on the Overview tab.

  3. Each measurement type made on the sample has its own table, with a heading naming the type and the number of its measurements. The most numerous type comes first, and the tables follow one another on the page, so you can read the results of different methods together.

  4. The column after the name gives each measurement’s dataset. It is a link when you may open the dataset. A measurement can belong to a different dataset from its sample.

  5. Select a column heading to sort that table. A table with more than fifty measurements has its own pager, and moving through one table leaves the others where they are.

The tab lists the measurements whose own dataset you may see. Being on the team of the sample’s dataset does not show you a measurement in another team’s unpublished or private dataset. The tab has no search and no filters. A sample with no measurements you may see still has the tab, and it says there are none. The tab opens for the people who may open the sample, and its address answers “not found” to anyone else.

Find records of a type the portal no longer registers#

When a dataset holds records of a type the portal has stopped registering, the heading menu has one more entry, Other. It lists them in a basic table with the columns every sample, or every measurement, has. On a sample’s Measurements tab they come last, in a table headed Measurements.

When a tab is missing#

A tab is shown only to people who may see the dataset’s records.

  • A private dataset is closed to strangers, with all its tabs.

  • A public dataset that is not published opens for everyone, but its records open only to its team. Everyone else sees no Samples or Measurements tab and no sample or measurement figure on the Overview, and the tab’s address answers “not found”.

  • A dataset in a private project is closed to anyone without a level on the project or the dataset.

  • A private project is closed to strangers, with all its tabs.

A person’s or organization’s page always has both tabs. They list only what you may see.

A dataset that holds no samples still has the tab. It says that the dataset has none.

Why the figures and the tabs agree#

The figures on the Overview of a dataset or a project, its charts and the tabs all count the records you may see, so the number on a figure is the number you find when you open the tab. The same holds for the number of datasets on a project. A person with more access sees larger numbers than a visitor does. See How access to a record is decided.
